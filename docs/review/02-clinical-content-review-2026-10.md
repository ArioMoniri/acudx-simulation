Reviewers: board-certified emergency physician and critical-care nurse educator (dual-persona review).
Scope: `server/case_fixtures.py`, `server/condition_catalog.py`, `server/case_generator.py` (lines 375 to 900), `web/src/domain/treatments.ts`, `web/src/domain/clinicalCourse.ts`, `web/src/domain/orderCatalog.ts`, `web/src/domain/orderables.ts`, `web/src/domain/diagnosisCatalog.ts`, `web/src/domain/scopeGuard.ts`, `web/src/data/seedCases.ts`, `web/src/data/rooms.ts`, `web/src/components/room/*`, `web/src/lib/guidanceEngine.ts`.
Rules enforced: `docs/process/AGENT-PROTOCOL.md` section 2, in particular rule 2 (every clinical fact shown to a learner carries a citation that resolves and was verified against the source text) and rule 5 (education only, synthetic patients).
Source was read only; nothing was edited. Line numbers refer to the working tree at the time of review.

Severity scale:
- critical: would mis-educate a learner on a safety-relevant point, or models an unsafe action as acceptable
- major: clinically wrong or materially unrealistic; must be fixed before a clinician signs the content off
- minor: imprecision, inconsistency or missing nuance

## 2. Nurse-educator section: interprofessional realism

### 2.1 What each role should be able to do and see

**Physician (doctor seat)**
- Sees: full chart, all results, live monitor, MAR, prehospital record, nursing assessments and escalations, team floor.
- Does: history and exam, differential, orders (diagnostics and medications with dose, route, rate, indication), procedures, consults, disposition, physician-to-physician handoff (I-PASS), debrief leadership.
- Currently: can do most of this, but orders are dose-less chips, and the solo encounter lets the physician "administer" directly with no nurse in the loop (OrdersTab.tsx:434-472). In a team room this bypass should be disabled so that the order-to-administration loop runs through the nurse.

**Nurse (nurse seat)**
- Sees: triage data, live monitor and trends, the active order queue, MAR with last-dose times, allergy band, fluid balance, pain scores, the prehospital handover, early-warning score.
- Does: triage with ESI assignment; nurse-initiated protocol orders (12-lead ECG, POC glucose, IV access, oxygen titration, urine hCG, standard bloods); five-rights verification and allergy check before administration; independent double check for high-alert drugs (insulin, opioids, heparin, potassium, adrenaline infusions); administration documentation; reassessment at defined intervals (pain 30 min after analgesia, vitals per acuity); escalation by SBAR; clarification or refusal of an unsafe order (wrong dose, documented allergy, contraindicated per vitals); patient and family communication.
- Currently: the nurse can claim and complete tasks and post a canned SBAR line. There is no triage step, no protocol ordering, no MAR, no allergy or dose check, no reassessment prompt, no mechanism to refuse or query an order (OrderHandoffPanel.tsx, roomCaseStore.ts:528-575).

**Paramedic (paramedic seat)**
- Sees: scene information, mechanism, prehospital vitals series, prehospital interventions and drug doses given with times, 12-lead ECG acquired in the field, pre-alert status.
- Does: scene assessment, prehospital treatment within the national scope (oxygen, aspirin, GTN, naloxone, IM adrenaline, salbutamol, glucose or glucagon, IV fluids, CPR and defibrillation, 12-lead acquisition and transmission), pre-alert, ATMIST handover at the door, then the prehospital record is merged into the ED chart and MAR.
- Currently: one button that posts a fixed sentence, and the triage vitals shown are the ED vitals rather than a prehospital series (PrehospitalPanel.tsx:27-36).

### 2.2 Handoff structure the room should implement

**Paramedic to ED team (ATMIST)**: Age and sex; Time of onset or injury; Mechanism or presenting complaint; Injuries or illness found; Signs (vitals series, GCS, blood glucose, 12-lead); Treatment given with doses and times. The receiver reads back the critical items. Fields should be auto-filled from the case and the paramedic's logged actions, with free-text additions, and graded for completeness against the ground truth.

**Nurse escalation (SBAR)**: Situation (patient, location, the concern in one sentence); Background (working diagnosis, relevant history, allergies, treatments given); Assessment (current vitals from `currentVitals`, trend from `courseStatus`, early-warning score, the nurse's judgement); Recommendation (what is being requested and by when, for example "I need you at the bedside within 5 minutes"). The physician's acknowledgement should close the loop with a read-back of the request.

**Physician to physician or shift change (I-PASS)**: Illness severity (stable, watcher, unstable); Patient summary; Action list with owners and times; Situation awareness and contingency plans ("if BP falls below 90, then..."); Synthesis by receiver (read-back and questions). The room's existing `loop: open/closed` and `lockedToken` read-back mechanism (rooms.ts, roomStore.ts:736-770) is a sound foundation for the synthesis step.

### 2.3 What the room model lacks (prioritised)

1. A medication administration record with drug, dose, route, time, administering role and allergy check, shared by paramedic, nurse and physician (finding 9, 25).
2. Nurse-initiated protocol orders and a triage step with ESI assignment that is scored against the catalog acuity (finding 24).
3. Structured, auto-populated ATMIST, SBAR and I-PASS forms replacing canned sentences (finding 26).
4. The right to query or refuse an unsafe order, with the engine recognising a correct refusal (for example GTN at systolic 86) as a positive nursing action rather than a delay.
5. Reassessment prompts tied to interventions (pain score 30 minutes after analgesia, SpO2 after oxygen change, glucose 15 minutes after dextrose) and an early-warning score on the monitor.
6. A prehospital phase with its own clock and vitals series, so the paramedic's handover contains real content and the ED team can detect omissions.
7. Patient weight visible to all roles and used for weight-based dosing checks (weights already exist in seedCases but are unused).
8. Allergy variants in the case corpus (currently every seed and every templated case is NKDA), so the allergy check can ever fail.
9. Closed-loop timing metrics (order to acknowledgement, acknowledgement to administration, result to physician review) surfaced in the debrief.
10. A debrief that separates individual role competence (nurse monitoring and escalation, paramedic handover, physician reasoning) from team outcomes, which the `DebriefMark` type gestures at but the data model does not yet support.

## 4. Per-condition clinician sign-off checklist (required before a condition is learner-visible)

A condition is learner-visible only when every item is initialled by a licensed clinician reviewer (physician for items 1 to 9 and 14 to 17; nurse educator for items 10 to 13 and 18 to 20), with the citation URL recorded next to each fact, per AGENT-PROTOCOL section 2 rule 2.

**Epidemiology and coding**
1. ICD-10-CM leaf code, ICD-11 MMS code and (if psychiatric) DSM-5-TR code verified against the current tabular and the WHO browser, identical in `condition_catalog.py` and `diagnosisCatalog.ts`.
2. Sex constraint and age band set and plausible for the condition; paediatric conditions carry paediatric vitals and weight-based dosing.

**Presentation**
3. Chief complaint and HPI answers are in the patient's own voice and contain only information the patient could know; no laboratory, ECG or exam terms in patient speech.
4. Past medical history, medications (including anticoagulants and devices) and allergies are coherent with the condition and with every lab value (an INR of 2.6 implies an anticoagulant on the list).
5. Red flags are split into patient-reported symptoms and clinician-detected signs or results, each with a citation.

**Objective data**
6. Triage vitals and trajectory direction match the condition's physiology (bradycardia for heart block, hypertension above 180/120 for hypertensive emergency, core temperature below 35 or above 40 where the condition requires), stay within ESI danger-zone plausibility, and include temperature movement where relevant.
7. Every lab has the correct LOINC code for the analyte and unit, a sex- and age-appropriate reference interval, a unit consistent with the client orderables panel, and a qualitative field for non-numeric results; time-since-event is present where interpretation needs it (paracetamol, troponin, hCG).
8. Imaging modality is the one a clinician would order first, the finding is specific (not a diagnosis label), and any "do not delay treatment for imaging" rule is encoded as a contraindicated action.
9. ESI level follows the handbook decision points (ESI 1 only when an immediate life-saving intervention is required); disposition is condition-specific (operating room, stroke unit, CCU, ICU, ward, observation, discharge) and justified.

**Treatments and effects**
10. Each indicated medication carries drug, dose (adult and weight-based where relevant), route, rate or frequency, and the condition under which it is indicated; first-line routes are correct (for example IM adrenaline for anaphylaxis).
11. Each contraindicated action is genuinely contraindicated per a cited guideline (not merely non-preferred), and at least one contraindicated item tests a common real-world error for that condition.
12. Effect magnitudes and onset times are pharmacologically plausible (no 30-minute antipyresis from antibiotics, no analgesia from aspirin, no 15-minute pain relief from PCI activation); cumulative and adverse effects of repeat dosing are modelled for high-alert drugs.
13. Rescue logic in `clinicalCourse.ts` derives from the authored indicated treatments, and contraindicated treatments produce a negative physiologic effect or a scored error, never a rescue.

**Guidance and citations**
14. Every guideline anchor and guidance passage names its true source with a URL or DOI that resolves and whose text the reviewer has read; no in-house passages carry a third-party attribution.
15. Thresholds quoted (time windows, BP targets, lab cut-offs) match the cited guideline edition and the edition year is recorded.
16. Turnaround labels for the condition's expected orders are realistic and state their measurement endpoint.
17. Differentials in the seed or generated case include the dangerous mimics for that complaint and at least one low-value order is marked as such where a guideline says not to order it.

**Interprofessional content**
18. Nurse-initiated protocol actions, reassessment intervals and escalation triggers for this condition are specified and scorable (SBAR content fields defined).
19. Prehospital actions appropriate to the condition are listed for the paramedic seat with doses, and the ATMIST fields for the case are populated.
20. A five-rights and allergy check is possible for every medication in the case (patient identity, drug, dose, route, time; at least one case variant in the condition family has a documented relevant allergy).

**Sign-off record**: reviewer name and credential, date, commit hash of the content reviewed, list of citation URLs checked, and any items waived with a written justification. Content without a complete record stays behind the degraded or "unverified" flag in the UI.
